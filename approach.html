APPROACH

Operating at the intersection of data, society, and the more-than-human, we begin with context.

Data is never simply information. It emerges from people, institutions, technologies, environments, organisms, places, and histories. A dataset can be produced by a person, a sensor, a plant, a landscape, a machine, or the interaction between them. What becomes visible depends on what is observed, how it is collected, and what remains outside the frame.

We work with stakeholders to understand these conditions before deciding what the data should become. We listen to local knowledge, institutional memory, lived experience and existing records, bringing different forms of evidence into relation with one another.

We look for the signal without losing the context.

A pattern can emerge from a dataset, but its meaning may sit somewhere else: in an archive, a conversation, a material practice, an image, a place or a piece of knowledge that was never formally recorded. We use data-led and experimental methods to find these connections, identify gaps and make relationships visible.

The method follows the question.

Sometimes that means structuring and analysing a dataset. Sometimes it means mapping, documenting, visualising, archiving or working across images, sound, text and other forms of evidence. Sometimes the most important part is creating a space for people to interpret the information themselves.

The final form should serve the knowledge, not the other way around.

We translate complex material into forms that can be understood, shared and used, while keeping its origins and relationships intact. The result might be a report, archive, map, visualisation, digital tool, exhibition or something that does not yet have a name.

Our role is not to speak for the data.

It is to create the conditions for its different signals, perspectives and stories to be heard.
